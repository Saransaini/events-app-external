Rev B · supersedes the manifold design

VAULT Decision Record

Every architectural decision behind the enclosure, what it was chosen over, what it costs, and how confident we are in it — plus the ones still open, and the rules that any future change has to respect. Companion to the full engineering document.

PART 1

The architecture, on one page

V-10 Studio shown · no internal plumbing

Four subsystems, and only four. The frame is a single connected air volume; the membrane is two layers on one tensioning system; the air system is one valve; everything else is an accessory that clips to the first two.

The one sentence version

Three inflated arches hold a net tight; the net holds the arches steady. Everything else is a consequence of those two facts.

PART 2

Decisions in force

Twelve · each one changeable, none of them free

Confidence is stated plainly on each: calculated means hand analysis that has not been tested, reasoned means a judgement with no number behind it, measured means a rig proved it. Nothing here is measured yet.

D-01

Frame geometry

Active
Chose
Pneumatic arch frame
Over
Inflated portal box (the reference architecture) · A-frame hybrid
Because
An inflated tube fails by wrinkling in bending but is excellent in axial compression. An arch converts the load into thrust along the tube. Ø203 at SF 2.76 does the work a portal would need Ø332 for — roughly 38 % less frame fabric.
Costs
No rectilinear corners to mount anything to, and curved tubes need mitred-segment patterning rather than straight cylinders.
Confidence
Calculated — wrinkling-moment and funicular analysis. Test T1 settles it.
D-02

Arch profile

Active
Chose
Three-centred (basket-handle): R₁ = 1.25W, R₂ = 0.31W
Over
Semicircular · parabolic · single-radius segmental
Because
A single-radius arch peaks at a point; a golf club’s apex wanders about ±750 mm with the player’s height and plane, so a point-peaked arch clips the club off centre. The flat crown band holds full height across ±898 mm and only drops away at the haunches, where the club is near vertical.
Costs
More complex geometry and more mitre joints than a single radius.
Confidence
Geometric, and cheap to check — chalk it full size and swing a driver inside it before cutting anything.
D-03

Tube section

Active
Chose
Round welded tube at 12 psi
Over
Drop-stitch flat panel (D-section) at 15–20 psi
Because
For a pressure-stabilised member a circle’s effective section modulus (πR³) beats a rectangle’s (bh²/6) by about 3× at equal depth. Drop-stitch earns its cost when you need bending capacity — and the arch is precisely what removes the bending demand.
Costs
No flat face to bond hardware to, so the net attachment needs a welded land strip along the arch.
Confidence
Calculated. Drop-stitch stays on the table for the base rail alone, where a flat top would resist a topped iron better.
D-04

Role of the net

Active
Chose
Pre-tensioned structural diaphragm
Over
Hanging liner carried by the frame (the reference architecture)
Because
It braces the arches out of plane, spreads a point impact across all three instead of one (SF 1.3 → 2.8 at the same tube size), gives the screen a flat plane for free, and closes the load loop inside the structure so nothing reaches the floor — no stakes or ballast indoors.
Costs
The frame now carries membrane tension, and the net must be tensioned at setup and re-tensioned once it relaxes.
Confidence
Calculated. This is the load-bearing idea of the whole design and the least proven — T1 and T3 exist for it.
D-05

Net construction

Active
Chose
Two layers — taut structural diagrid behind, slack arrest net in front
Over
A single net doing both jobs
Because
The two jobs are irreconcilable in one layer: tight enough to brace the frame is far too tight to catch a ball gently. They also want opposite fibres — the diagrid must not stretch (polyester), the arrest net must (nylon, where the elongation is the energy absorption).
Costs
Two layers to manufacture, fit and service.
Confidence
Reasoned, and hard to argue with — the conflict is real whatever the numbers turn out to be.
D-06

Diagrid orientation

Active
Chose
45° bias to the principal tension, 600 mm pitch
Over
Square grid aligned to the frame
Because
A square mesh loaded in its own plane is a mechanism — it racks into parallelograms and carries nothing until it locks up. On the bias the load runs axially down the webs instead.
Costs
More webbing length and more offcut for the same coverage.
Confidence
Calculated. T3 (2 kN in-plane shear, racking ≤ 2°) is the test that proves the net is structure and not cargo.
D-07

Air distribution

Active · replaced D-07a
Chose
Ø50 open transfer ports at every joint — one connected air space, one valve
Over
Internal manifold hose with per-limb check valves (D-07a) · a separate valve on every chamber
Because
The manifold bought tolerance to a single-chamber puncture, and charged ten parts, about eight pounds, extra weld operations and eight more joints that can leak. Redundancy that adds eight failure points to guard against one is not redundancy.
Costs
A puncture anywhere deflates the whole cage. Accepted: nothing shelters under it, and at 12 psi a small hole sinks it over minutes. Patch and re-inflate.
Confidence
Reasoned. T6 now checks port sizing rather than redundancy — that the far purlin does not lag the valve during inflation.
D-08

Gauge location

Active
Chose
On the pump
Over
Integral to the fill valve on the product
Because
Pressure only needs reading while you are pumping. A gauge on the cage is a permanent leak path bought for no occasion when you would look at it.
Costs
You cannot check pressure without getting the pump out.
Confidence
Reasoned.
D-09

Arrest travel

Active
Chose
500 mm design travel, field-adjustable 250–650 mm at the tensioners
Over
A fixed, factory-set net tension
Because
Peak force goes as 1/δ, so travel is the design variable, not a nuisance: 500 mm gives 734 N and SF 2.76. Making it adjustable lets an owner trade a harder catch for a shallower room.
Costs
Requires 700 mm of standoff from the wall, and it is possible for an owner to tighten into SF 1.55 without realising.
Confidence
Calculated on a linear-spring model. T2 measures the real curve.
D-10

Screen mounting

Active
Chose
Laced to the structural diagrid on shock cord
Over
Its own tensioning frame · direct attachment to the arch
Because
The diagrid is already a flat, pre-tensioned plane, so the screen inherits flatness instead of generating it — and with no rigid frame edge there is no abrasion line, which is where simulator screens normally fail first.
Costs
Screen flatness is now hostage to diagrid tension; slack net means a slack picture.
Confidence
Reasoned. The claimed screen-life improvement has no data behind it and should stay out of marketing until T2 has screen samples in the rig.
D-11

Materials

Active
Chose
PVC-coated polyester tubes · nylon arrest net · polyester diagrid
Over
TPU-coated tubes · HDPE or polyester arrest net · nylon webbing
Because
PVC-coated polyester is HF-weldable, so any inflatable-boat shop can build it and any owner can patch it. The two net fibres are chosen for opposite reasons: nylon’s stretch is the arrest mechanism, polyester’s lack of stretch is the structure.
Costs
PVC creeps more than TPU and is heavier for equal strength. A lighter TPU variant is the obvious second-generation move.
Confidence
Reasoned, and standard practice across the inflatable industry.
D-12

Packed form

Active · binding constraint
Chose
One shoulder-carried bag, every model
Over
Two bags split frame / net
Because
A portable product that takes two trips from the car is a different and worse product, whatever it weighs. The benchmark packs into one duffel; matching that is the entry ticket, not a bonus.
Costs
Constrains every future change — nothing may be added that pushes the V-10 past one bag.
Confidence
Calculated on packed volume (~140 L into a ~290 L duffel). Weigh and pack the first prototype before believing it.
PART 3

Superseded

Kept on the record deliberately

Reversed decisions are recorded rather than deleted, for two reasons. Anyone reading the design later will otherwise re-propose the same idea; and for patent purposes, what was tried and abandoned is part of the development history counsel may want.

D-07a

Internal manifold with per-limb check valves

Superseded
Was
A 25 mm hose routed inside the base rail, feeding each arch and purlin through a one-way umbrella check valve, so a puncture drained only the chamber it was in.
Dropped
Ten parts against one, ~4.3 kg, seven extra socket weld operations, and eight additional joints that can leak — to protect a garage product against a failure whose consequence is “stop hitting balls and patch it”.
Replaced by
D-07 — open Ø50 transfer ports.
Also lost
It was on the patent novelty list as a dependent claim. Withdrawn there too, and marked so counsel does not search for a feature the product no longer has. No real loss — multi-chamber inflation manifolds are well covered by inflatable-boat and airbeam-shelter prior art.
Revisit if
The product is ever taken outdoors as semi-permanent installation, or scaled to a size where re-inflating is a two-person job.
PART 4

Still open

Yours to decide — not engineering calls

Six decisions the design deliberately leaves unmade, because they are commercial or strategic rather than technical. Each one is stated with what it turns on.

O-01

Where to manufacture

Open
Options
Offshore at $662 landed · domestic at ~$1,019
Turns on
Whether a made-domestically story is worth $357 a unit to your market. The entire gap is labour: $42 against $315. Materials are near identical.
Note
Domestic manufacture cannot reach an $800 unit cost. If that target is firm, this decision is already made.
O-02

Which model launches first

Open
Options
V-9 Garage · V-10 Studio · V-12 Academy
Turns on
V-10 is the recommended first article: it is the honest full-driver product, the direct benchmark comparison, and the size the calculations were run at. V-9 reaches the larger market but ships with a swing-height caveat that has to be published honestly.
Note
Tooling is shared across the range, so this is a sequencing decision, not a fork.
O-03

Include the pump, or sell it separately

Open
Options
In the box · accessory
Turns on
$55 a unit — the single largest cost-down available and the most visible to a buyer. Competitors include one.
O-04

Retail price

Open
Options
$1,200 (45 % margin) · $1,500 (56 %) · $1,900 (65 %, room for dealers)
Turns on
Whether you sell direct or through retail. $800 is not viable as a selling price at a $662 cost — 17 % gross does not survive returns, warranty and freight damage.
O-05

Relief valve: keep or delete

Open
Options
Fit it · delete it and rely on the burst margin
Turns on
Physics does not need it — thermal rise takes 12 psi to about 14.7, against 60 psi minimum burst. It exists to protect against a customer who pumps to 20 and parks it in the sun. $6 at volume.
Note
Inflatable paddleboards ship without one. This is a product-liability judgement, not an engineering one.
O-06

File the provisional before or after the test rig

Open
Options
File first, then build · build the $780 arch rig first, then file
Turns on
Filing first buys a priority date on an architecture that has not been proven. Building first risks someone else filing in the interval, but means you file on something you know works — and the test may change the claims.
Note
These are not exclusive. A provisional is cheap and can be filed on the current disclosure while the rig is built, with the non-provisional written from test data within the year.
PART 5

Rules any future change must respect

The constraints, not the preferences

Everything above can be revisited. These cannot be quietly traded away — each one is either a safety floor or the thing that makes the product a product. If a proposed change breaks one, the change is wrong, not the rule.

ConstraintLimitWhy it is a floor
Combined arch safety factor≥ 2.5Wrinkling is a sudden loss of stiffness, not a gradual one. Below about 2 the frame has no reserve for a mis-hit or a lean.
Fabric burst margin≥ 4:1Industry norm for pressurised fabric structures. Currently 5.7:1 — the headroom that lets you consider lighter fabric later.
Packed form1 bag, 1 personTwo trips from the car makes it a different product. D-12.
Wall standofftravel + 200 mmIf the net can reach the wall, the wall arrests the ball. Non-negotiable and must be printed in the manual.
Frame clear of the swingno member inside the club envelopeA club strike on a pressurised tube at 100 mph is a burst, not a scuff.
Arrest netfield-replaceableIt is a consumable at 100,000 impacts. If replacing it means replacing the cage, the product has a one-season life.
Published swing ratinghonest per modelThe V-9 is height-limited and saying so costs sales. Overstating it costs returns and reviews, which cost more.
