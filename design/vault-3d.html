The safety factor, specifically

You asked where 2.5 came from. From me. There is no standard for inflatable golf cages. The nearest ones — EN 14960 and ASTM F2374 — cover bouncy castles and don’t apply.

The reasoning: fabric structures normally use 4:1 or 5:1 against bursting, because a burst is sudden and dangerous. This design has 5.1:1 there. But 2.5 is against wrinkling — a different failure where the tube goes soft and sags rather than exploding. A lower number is defensible for a gentler failure mode, and 2.5 is a common range in inflatable-structure practice.

But it is a judgement, not a requirement. If you told an engineer “use 3.0” they could not point to a rule saying you’re wrong — the tubes would just get bigger.

The one that should worry you

That 45% load sharing figure. When a ball hits the net, the net spreads the shock between the two arches — and I assumed the worse-loaded one takes 45% of it. I have no evidence for that number. If it’s really 70%, the safety factor drops from 2.8 to about 1.6 and the arches need to grow.

Everything structural rests on it, and it is exactly what test T3 measures. Do not commit to tooling before that test. It is also most of why the single-arch rig is the right first $780 to spend.
